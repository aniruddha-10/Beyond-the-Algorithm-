Findings

What we figured out along the way

Four patterns that stood out in our 356 ads, a few smaller surprises, and what we learned from collecting the data ourselves.

01 · Relevance vs. intrusiveness

The better an ad fit, the more it felt too personal.

Not one of the 123 ads we rated 1 felt intrusive. Two-thirds of the ads we rated 5 did.

Those same top-rated ads were also the ones we acted on: we clicked 46% of them, against 8% of ads overall.

Relevance cuts both ways: the ads that work best sit closest to the line.

02 · Targeting

Almost half our ads had nothing to do with us.

For 173 of 356 ads (49%), the person who saw it couldn't name any reason they'd been shown it. Those ads averaged 1.5 out of 5 for relevance.

For all the talk of algorithms that know everything, a big share of what reached us looked untargeted.

03 · Why we thought we saw it

How they seemed to know mattered more than what they knew.

Ads we linked to a product we already use rated 3.6 for relevance, yet only 1 of 38 felt intrusive.

Ads that appeared right after we talked or messaged about something rated 4.0, and 6 of 7 felt too personal. That group is small, so treat it as a hint rather than proof.

Being recognised as a customer felt fine. Feeling overheard did not.

04 · Platforms

Same aim, very different reactions.

On both platforms, exactly 53.4% of ads matched one of our listed interests. Instagram and YouTube were equally on target.

Yet we clicked 11.1% of Instagram ads and only 3.4% of YouTube ads, more than three times the rate.

Lessons from the process

Collecting it ourselves taught us a few things too.

01

Brand names are messy.

Our log had 263 different brand spellings. Once we fixed typos and variants (Grokbot → Grok, Bet366 → Bet365), they came down to 211 brands.

02

One person, a third of the data.

P1 logged 115 of the 356 ads. Instead of throwing data away, we added a switch that counts each person equally. Most results barely moved: average relevance went from 2.53 to 2.47.

03

“Why did I see this?” is a guess.

Platforms don't tell us what data they used, so every reason here is our own perception. These findings describe how targeting felt to five people over 24 days, not how it works.
